CI-04 Crawl space depth and the FEMA flood rules Open

Status
Work-around built (owner-reported); the crawl floor after the fill and the pad tops are not measured.
Responsible (as reported)
Elevate (owner's view)
Found
2026-05-11

Plans and rules: the house is in a flood zone. FEMA's crawl space rules (Technical Bulletin 11) limit how far the crawl space floor may sit below the outside ground, and the flood permit sets the lowest floor at 1047.5 ft. The plan sheets give no crawl floor elevation and no flood elevation. The S4.1 wall sections give only limits: at least 1'-6" from the floor joists down to the crawl space dirt, the wall at least 6" above the outside ground, and the bottom of the footing 18" to 4'-0" below the outside ground. A02's building section draws the foundation with no dimension below the lower floor.

Built: on 2026-05-11 the crawl space dirt was about 1043.0 ft (field), about 6" past the FEMA limits. The interior footing pads had already been poured on that dirt, about 8-12" above it. The owner says 1 ft of backfill was then added, leaving the pads too low, and he built forms around them to extend them up. The interior and exterior backfill was site work, which the owner arranged directly, outside Elevate's contract.

Who is responsible: the owner's view is that Alex "didn't consider some of the FEMA flood regulations when they poured the concrete". No document on file assigns the dig depth, the crawl floor elevation or flood compliance to anyone:

  • the plan sheets give the depth limits above, but no crawl floor elevation and no flood elevation;
  • Elevate's contract is to build "according to approved plans", with the permits and workmanlike work. Attachment A lists the foundation as "Per permit drawings" and site work as "Not included". Neither names the dig depth, the crawl floor or the flood rules;
  • the 1047.5 ft lowest floor appears only in the county flood permit.

Who dug the crawl space to its depth is not on record.

History:

  • 2026-05-08: the owner's line-level check of the form tops from the surveyor's benchmark read the top of the board he measured at 1047.5 ft, the design top of wall (see CI-01).
  • 2026-05-11: Lewis County inspector Neal Bahrt told the owner verbally that the county has no requirement to backfill the crawl space, but warned that flood insurance can be affected if the crawl floor sits too far below the outside ground. The same day, the owner's measurements put the crawl dirt about 6" past both TB-11 limits, with the pads already poured, and the rules were worked through in a research session.
  • 2026-05-12: The county flood code, LCC 15.35, was read in full: it has no rule that piers stand 8" above grade. The owner reported that Neal had already passed the forms and the poured pads.
  • 2026-05-13: the flood-insurance inspection analysis weighed how likely a later check of the crawl floor is.
  • 2026-06-02: Anderson Dirtworks was paid $5,280.00 for the general interior and exterior backfill. The owner says this was not a repair cost for this issue.
  • 2026-08-13: the owner confirmed that the drafted emails to Neal Bahrt and to Pieruccioni about the pad extensions were never sent. The email asking Butler Surveying how the elevation certificate will measure the crawl floor is still open.
  • Field facts: 1 ft of fill was added, and the owner built forms around the pads to extend them.

Fix: the owner's pad extensions. On paper, a full 12" of fill would turn the 6" shortfall into about a 6" margin on both TB-11 limits (the Option 2 row on the crawl space study), if the measured crawl floor and outside ground bear that out.

Evidence still needed

  1. The crawl floor height after the fill. Quick route: the owner tapes from the top of the stem wall down to the crawl floor at 3-4 spots, and down to the outside ground on each side. Official route: Butler Surveying measures the crawl floor (C2.a) and the lowest outside ground (LAG) for the post-construction elevation certificate.
  2. Photos of the pad extensions, if any exist.
  3. Any message with Alex or his crew about the dig depth or the flood rules.
