Reading the pictures

A render is a photograph of the drawing, not of the house: the shape comes from the same measured model as the interactive drawing, and the light is a real sky. What it shows well is proportion, how a finish reads at its real size, how colours sit together and how the light falls. What it cannot show yet is the actual material, because most finishes are free look-alike textures standing in for samples not yet photographed.
  • Option sheets — two or three pictures of one view that differ in exactly one finish, side by side, with the changed surface named under each panel. Everything else in the panels is identical, so the difference you see is the option.
  • Decoration — bowls, boards, plants and baskets are staging and are named as such. Stools and the dining table are drawn to size but not chosen; the captions call them placeholders.
  • The view — the north windows show the owner's own photograph of Mount Rainier from the lot, stretched wider than its true angle; every other window shows a stock sky image, not this site.
  • Decisions — nothing on this page is a decision. Finish choices are recorded in the project wiki once made.
Not engineered — do not build from these. The renders are pictures of drawings that interpret the Armstrong Design permit set (project 2507) and field conditions the owner has recorded; finishes are stand-ins until the samples are photographed. Every picture carries a receipt naming the drawing, finishes, cameras and light that made it.
